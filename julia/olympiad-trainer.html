03 Introduction · Exam tactics

How to score more points

  • Read the whole sentence first. The answer often depends on a word at the end.
  • Open cloze = grammar words (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, pronouns) — almost never big content words.
  • Word formation: decide the part of speech first (noun? adjective? adverb?), then add the ending — and check for a negative prefix.
  • Never leave a gap empty. A guess can score; a blank never does.
  • Watch spelling. recieve, succesful, writting = wrong = zero points.

05 Rules · Multiple-choice traps

What the four options are testing

Grammar signals to spot

  • Tense words: since / for / already → Perfect; ago / yesterday → Past Simple
  • Reported speech: no do/did, normal word order (…where I lived)
  • Quantifiers: much + uncountable, many + countable
  • Conditionals: If I were you…

Vocabulary signals

  • Collocations: make a decision, do homework
  • Dependent prepositions: good at, afraid of, depend on
  • Phrasal verbs: give up, look after, take off
  • Similar words: lend / borrow, say / tell, lie / lay
⚠️ Distractor trick → two options often look almost right (been / gone, much / many). The wrong one usually breaks a small rule — number, tense, or preposition.

10 Rules · Word formation

Suffix & prefix toolbox

Make a noun

  • -tion / -sion: decide → decision, act → action
  • -ment: develop → development
  • -ness: happy → happiness · -ity: able → ability
  • -er / -or / -ist: teach → teacher, science → scientist

Make an adjective

  • -ful / -less: care → careful / careless
  • -ous: danger → dangerous · -al: nature → natural
  • -ive: create → creative · -y: rain → rainy
  • -able / -ible: comfort → comfortable, sense → sensible

Make an adverb / verb

  • -ly: quick → quickly · (careful → carefully)
  • -en / -ise / -ify: wide → widen, modern → modernise

Negative prefixes

  • im- (m,p): impossible · il- (l): illegal
  • ir- (r): irregular · in-: incorrect
  • un- / dis- / mis-: unfair, dishonest, misuse
⚠️ Two things to check every time → ① Is the word plural? (inventions) ② Does the meaning need a negative? "It is ___ to fly without wings" (POSSIBLE) → impossible, not possible.

13 Rules · Error correction

The mistakes olympiads plant

Grammar errors

  • Double comparative: more taller → taller
  • Subject–verb: There is many people → are
  • Wrong verb pattern: suggest to go → suggest going
  • Tense clash: have seen him yesterday → saw

Word-choice errors

  • say me → tell me · said me → told me
  • I am agree → I agree
  • depends of → depends on
  • interesting in → interested in
⚠️ Russian-influence errors → I have 14 years → I am 14 · make a photo → take a photo · in the last time → recently.
14 Task · Error correction

Correct the wrong word

  • 1. ❌ He is more taller than me → He is than me.
  • 2. ❌ I am agree with you → I with you.
  • 3. ❌ She said me the news → She me the news.
  • 4. ❌ There is many people here → There many people here.
  • 5. ❌ I look forward to see you → I look forward to you.
  • 6. ❌ He suggested to go home → He suggested home.
  • 7. ❌ It depends of the weather → It depends the weather.
  • 8. ❌ I have 14 years old → I 14 years old.
15 Rules · Phrasal verbs & prepositions

Learn them as fixed pairs

Common phrasal verbs

  • give up = quit · look after = take care of
  • take off = plane leaves / remove · put off = postpone
  • look forward to = anticipate · find out = discover
  • take after = resemble · put up with = tolerate

Dependent prepositions

  • good at · afraid of · interested in
  • depend on · listen to · wait for
  • proud of · married to · famous for
  • congratulate on · apologise for
⚠️ Verbs with NO preposition → enter a room, discuss a plan, answer a question, marry someone, reach a place — no enter into / discuss about / answer to.

17 Rules · Idioms & collocations

Idioms that love to appear

Everyday idioms

  • a piece of cake = very easy
  • once in a blue moon = very rarely
  • under the weather = feeling unwell
  • hit the books = study hard
  • break a leg! = good luck!
  • let the cat out of the bag = reveal a secret

make vs do

  • make: a mistake, a decision, progress, friends, noise, money, an effort
  • do: homework, the shopping, a favour, your best, the washing-up, exercise
⚠️ Idioms are fixed → it's always raining cats and dogs, never cats and mice; break a leg, never break an arm. Learn the exact words.

20 Task · Reading (True / False / Not stated)

Read, then decide

The Eden Project

The Eden Project in Cornwall, England, is one of the most unusual attractions in the country. It was built inside an old clay pit and opened to the public in 2001. Its most famous features are two huge domes, called "biomes", which look like giant bubbles.

Inside them, thousands of plants from all over the world grow in carefully controlled conditions. One biome recreates a hot, wet rainforest, while the other copies the warm, dry climate of the Mediterranean. The project was designed not only to entertain visitors but also to teach them about nature and the importance of protecting it.

Every year, more than a million people come to explore the biomes, walk through the gardens, and learn how humans and plants depend on each other.

21 Facts · The United Kingdom 🇬🇧

Must-know UK facts

The basics

  • Full name: United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland
  • Capital: London · Currency: pound sterling (£)
  • 4 parts: England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland
  • Monarch (2026): King Charles III
  • Flag: the Union Jack (Union Flag)

Symbols & sights

  • England → rose · Scotland → thistle
  • Wales → daffodil / leek · N. Ireland → shamrock
  • PM's home: 10 Downing Street
  • Parliament: House of Commons + House of Lords
  • Big Ben = the bell; the tower is the Elizabeth Tower
⚠️ Easy to mix up → Great Britain = the island (England + Scotland + Wales). The UK = Great Britain + Northern Ireland. They are not the same!
22 Facts · The USA & beyond 🇺🇸

USA & the English-speaking world

The USA

  • Capital: Washington, D.C. · Currency: US dollar ($)
  • 50 states · flag = Stars and Stripes (50 stars, 13 stripes)
  • First president: George Washington
  • Independence Day: 4 July (1776)
  • Statue of Liberty — a gift from France

Other capitals to know

  • Australia → Canberra (not Sydney!)
  • Canada → Ottawa · symbol = maple leaf
  • New Zealand → Wellington
  • Ireland → Dublin
⚠️ Famous "traps" → the biggest US city is New York, but the capital is Washington, D.C. · Australia's biggest city is Sydney, but the capital is Canberra.

24 Rules + task · Writing

Structure & linkers that score

Opinion paragraph plan

  • 1 Opinion: In my opinion… / I strongly believe…
  • 2 Reason + example: Firstly… For instance…
  • 3 Other side: However, some people say…
  • 4 Conclusion: All in all… / To sum up…

Linkers toolbox

firstlymoreoverin additionhoweveralthoughfor examplesuch asthereforeas a resultto sum up
