02 Speaking · Warm-up · ~7 min

How do you measure things? 🗣️

Answer out loud in full sentences. Don't worry about the exact numbers — the words are what matter.

  • How tall are you and how much do you weigh? Now try to say it the American way — in feet, inches and pounds!
  • How far is it from your home to your school? Say it in kilometres, then in miles (1 mile ≈ 1.6 km).
  • What's the temperature outside today? Would an American say the same number?
  • Look at a recipe you know. Which measuring words does it use — spoons, cups, grams, "to taste"?
  • Have you ever seen a British or American product with strange units on the label (oz, lb, fl oz, °F)?
  • Why do you think the USA has never changed to the metric system?

04 Theory · Two systems

Metric vs imperial 🌍

The metric system (SI)

  • Length: mm · cm · m · km
  • Weight: mg · g · kg · tonne
  • Volume: ml · litre (BrE) / liter (AmE)
  • Area: m² · hectare · km²
  • Temperature: degrees Celsius (°C)
  • Everything is divided by ten → easy maths
  • Born in France after the Revolution (1790s)
used by ~95% of the world

Imperial & US customary

  • Length: inch · foot · yard · mile
  • Weight: ounce · pound · stone · ton
  • Volume: fl oz · pint · quart · gallon
  • Area: square foot · acre · square mile
  • Temperature: degrees Fahrenheit (°F)
  • Numbers are all different: 12, 14, 16, 1760…
  • Came from body parts and farming (a foot, a hand, a day's ploughing)
the USA · partly the UK
⚠️ Key olympiad fact → only three countries have not officially adopted the metric system: the USA, Liberia and Myanmar (Burma). The British system is called imperial; the American one is US customary — the names of the units are the same, but some sizes are not!

07 Theory · Weight & mass ⚖️

Ounces, pounds and stones

1 ounce (oz)= 28 gунция — маленькие продукты, письма
1 pound (lb)= 16 oz = 454 gфунт — почти полкило
1 stone (st)= 14 lb = 6.35 kgстоун — только вес человека, только в Британии
1 US (short) ton= 2,000 lb = 907 kgамериканская тонна
1 UK (long) ton= 2,240 lb = 1,016 kgбританская тонна
1 tonne / metric ton= 1,000 kgобычная тонна

Say it like a native

  • "Could I have two pounds of apples, please?"
  • "The baby weighed seven pounds four ounces."
  • "She weighs nine stone." (≈ 57 kg)
  • "It's sold by the kilo."
  • Verb to weigh — noun weight — tool: scales (BrE) / a scale (AmE)

Where the words come from

  • lb ← Latin libra (весы) — that's why "pound" is written lb!
  • oz ← Italian onza
  • stone ← literally a stone used on old market scales
  • Money: the pound sterling (£) was once a pound of silver — same word, different meaning
⚠️ Traps → after a number the word stone usually stays singular: "He weighs twelve stone." · ton ≠ tonne · a pound can be money or weight — context decides.
08 Theory · Length & distance 📏

Inches, feet, yards and miles

1 inch (in / ")= 2.54 cmдюйм — экраны, дождь, рост
1 foot (ft / ')= 12 inches = 30.5 cmфут — рост, высота
1 yard (yd)= 3 feet = 0.91 mярд — ткань, американский футбол
1 mile (mi)= 1,760 yards = 1.609 kmмиля — дороги
1 nautical mile= 1,852 mморская миля; скорость — в knots (узлах)

Talking about height

  • "I'm five foot six." = 5' 6" = 168 cm
  • "He's six feet two." / "He's six two."
  • Symbols: ' = feet, " = inches
  • How tall are you? — I'm 1 m 68. / I'm five six.

Foot or feet?

  • After a number, alone: feet — "The wall is six feet high."
  • Before a noun (compound adjective): foot, no -s — "a six-foot wall"
  • Same rule everywhere: a five-mile walk, a two-litre bottle, a ten-year-old boy
  • Distance question: How far is it? — It's about three miles.
⚠️ Remember → 1 mile ≈ 1.6 km and 1 km ≈ 0.6 mile. A "60 mph" speed limit is about 100 km/h. Rain and snow in the USA are measured in inches, TV and phone screens everywhere in inches.

Spoons, cups, pints and gallons

1 teaspoon (tsp)= 5 mlчайная ложка
1 tablespoon (tbsp)= 3 tsp = 15 mlстоловая ложка (в Австралии — 20 ml!)
1 fluid ounce (fl oz)≈ 30 mlжидкая унция

1 pint (US)= 16 fl oz = 473 mlамериканская пинта
1 pint (UK)= 20 fl oz = 568 mlбританская пинта — больше!

In real life → "A pint of beer, please." · "We need two pints of milk." · "Gas is four dollars a gallon." · "Add half a cup of sugar."
10 Theory · Area & land 🌾

Acres, hectares and square feet

1 square foot (sq ft)= 0.093 m²квадратный фут — площадь квартир в США/Британии
1 square yard= 9 sq ft = 0.84 m²ковры, ткань
1 acre= 4,840 sq yd = 4,047 m²акр — земля, поля, фермы
1 hectare (ha)= 10,000 m² = 2.47 acresгектар
1 square mile= 640 acres = 2.59 km²квадратная миля

Where the word "acre" comes from

  • An acre was the piece of land one man with one ox could plough in a single day.
  • Size in your head: about 0.4 hectare — a bit smaller than an American football field.
  • Farms in the USA, UK, Ireland, Canada and Australia are still often described in acres.

Say it right

  • "They own a 200-acre farm." (без -s перед существительным!)
  • "The park covers five acres."
  • "A 1,200-square-foot apartment."
  • Question: How big is it? / What's the area?
⚠️ Don't mix them up → an acre is area, but a furlong, a league and a fathom are length. A very common "odd one out" question!
11 Theory · Temperature 🌡️

Celsius vs Fahrenheit

What°C°F
Water freezes (замерзает)0 °C32 °F
A cold winter day−10 °C14 °F
A cool day10 °C50 °F

A hot summer day30 °C86 °F
Body temperature37 °C98.6 °F
Water boils (кипит)100 °C212 °F

The maths

  • °F = °C × 1.8 + 32
  • °C = (°F − 32) ÷ 1.8
  • Quick trick: double the °C and add 30 — close enough! (20 °C → 70 °F)

Say it right

  • "It's minus five." / "It's five below zero."
  • "It'll be in the eighties tomorrow." (80–89 °F)
  • "Preheat the oven to 200 degrees."
  • UK ovens also use gas mark 1–9 (gas mark 6 = 200 °C = 400 °F)
12 Theory · Bonus units for olympiads 🎩

Strange old measures you may meet

Still in use today

  • a hand = 4 inches — the height of horses («16 hands high»)
  • a furlong = 220 yards = ⅛ mile — horse racing
  • a fathom = 6 feet — depth of water
  • a knot = 1 nautical mile per hour — ships and planes
  • a carat = 0.2 g — diamonds
  • a dozen = 12 · a gross = 144

Mostly in books & idioms

  • a league ≈ 3 miles — "Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea"
  • a chain = 22 yards — the length of a cricket pitch
  • a bushel, a peck — old measures of grain and apples
  • a gill = ¼ pint — old measure for spirits
  • a score = 20 («four score and seven years ago»)
💡 Why this is worth learning → olympiad tasks love "odd one out" and "match the unit to what it measures". If you know that a hand is for horses and a fathom is for water, those are free points.

18 Theory · Halves & quarters ½

How to say the small amounts

Fractions (дроби)

  • ½ — a half → half a cup, half a teaspoon, half an hour
  • ⅓ — a third → a third of a litre
  • ¼ — a quarter → a quarter of a kilo
  • ¾ — three quarters → three quarters of a cup
  • 1½ — one and a half → one and a half hours
  • 2½ — two and a half → two and a half kilos

Word order & grammar

  • half a cup ✓   (не «a half cup» в британском английском)
  • a cup and a half = 1½ cups — оба варианта верны
  • После one and a half — множественное число: one and a half litres
  • Decimals: 2.5 = two point five · 0.5 = nought point five (BrE) / zero point five (AmE)
  • twice as much, three times as much, double the amount
Hedging words («примерно») → about · around · roughly · approximately · nearly · just over / just under · give or take · or so · -ish "It weighs about two kilos." · "Twenty minutes or so." · "Ten-ish."

When people don't measure at all

Small amounts of liquid

  • a drop of vanilla — капля

21 Theory · Grammar of quantity ⚠️

The traps olympiads love

No -s before a noun

  • a five-mile walk (не five-miles)
  • a two-litre bottle
  • a ten-year-old boy
  • a 200-acre farm
  • BUT alone, after the verb: "The walk is five miles long."

Numbers that never take -s

  • two dozen eggs (не dozens)
  • three hundred people · five thousand · two million
  • BUT: dozens of eggs, hundreds of people (без числа → -s + of)
  • twelve stone (BrE, body weight)

Much / many · little / few

  • much + uncountable: much sugar
  • many + countable: many apples
  • a little sugar (немного — положительно)
  • little sugar (мало — отрицательно)
  • a few apples / few apples — same difference
  • plenty of, a lot of — with both

Questions & verbs

  • How much flour? · How many eggs?
  • How far / How tall / How heavy / How deep / How wide?
  • Ten kilos is a lot. (мера = единое целое → is)
  • There is a lot of milk. / There are a lot of eggs.
  • Sold by the kilo, by weight, by the dozen

28 Task · Reading (True / False / Not stated)

Read, then decide

A country of two systems

Walk into a British supermarket and you will see prices in kilograms and grams, exactly as in the rest of Europe. Yet as soon as you step outside, the road signs tell you that the next town is twelve miles away and that the speed limit is thirty miles an hour. Britain began changing to the metric system in 1965, but the change was never completed, and today the country lives quite happily with both.

Some imperial units are even protected by law. Beer in a pub must be served in pints, and milk delivered to the door may be sold in pints too. Most British people still give their weight in stones and pounds and their height in feet and inches, although they were taught the metric system at school.

Younger people are more comfortable with metric units. Ask a teenager how tall she is and she may answer "168 centimetres", while her grandmother would say "five foot six". Petrol has been sold in litres since the 1980s, so almost nobody talks about gallons any more — except when they complain about the price.

Visitors from the United States find British measurements confusing for a different reason. An American pint is smaller than a British one, and an American gallon is smaller too. So a "gallon of milk" in New York is not the same amount as a "gallon of milk" in London.

  • 1. British supermarkets sell food in metric units.
  • 2. Britain finished changing to the metric system in 1965.
  • 3. British pubs may serve beer in half-litre glasses.
  • 4. Every British teenager uses only metric units.
  • 5. Petrol in Britain is sold in litres.
  • 6. An American gallon is bigger than a British one.
  • 7. The metric system is taught in British schools.
  • 8. Most Americans would like to change to the metric system.
