Theory · 1/10

few or little?

Two decisions: Can we count the noun? Then: Is the speaker satisfied with the amount?

countable plural → few uncountable → little
The article a changes the attitude, not the quantity.
Артикль меняет оценку говорящего: «недостаточно» ↔ «немного, но достаточно».
Theory · 2/10

few / a few + countable plural

−few = hardly any

not enough

Few students understood the rule.

Почти никто не понял. Это проблема.

+a few = some

enough / useful

A few students asked questions.

Несколько учеников задали вопросы — уже хорошо.

Test it: replace few with “almost no”, and a few with “some”. Does the meaning survive?
Theory · 3/10

little / a little + uncountable

−little = hardly any

not enough

We have little time.

Времени почти нет — нужно торопиться.

+a little = some

enough for something

We have a little time.

Немного времени есть — что-то успеем.

Common uncountables: time, money, information, advice, progress, work, luck, patience, traffic, furniture, research.

Theory · 5/10

Exceptions & traps

OF + determiner / pronoun

a few of the students

little of this information

Но: a few students — без of.

Not always quantity

I’m a little tired. = slightly

a little girl = small/young

Few can stand alone

Many applied, but few were accepted.

Few = few people/applicants.

Comparatives

fewer mistakes · less time

Countable plural → fewer; uncountable → less.

Fixed everyday exception: signs and measurements often use less with plurals: 10 items or less; less than 5 kilometres. In careful formal English, use fewer for countable items.
Theory · 6/10

a lot of / lots of / plenty of

All three work with countable plural and uncountable nouns.

Countable plural

a lot of books

lots of opportunities

Uncountable

a lot of time

lots of information

More than enough

We have plenty of time.

Времени много — его точно хватит.

A lot of is neutral. Lots of is more informal. Plenty of adds the idea “more than enough”.
Agreement: A lot of students are…, but A lot of information is… The noun controls the verb.
Theory · 7/10

fewer or less?

Fewer + countable plural

fewer mistakes

We made fewer mistakes today.

Можно посчитать: one mistake, two mistakes.

Less + uncountable

less information

This article gives less information.

Information не считаем поштучно.

Measurements behave as one amount: less than ten minutes, less than five kilometres, less than £20. With separate countable things, careful English prefers fewer than ten books.
Theory · 8/10

WordFocusGrammarExample
eachone by onesingular noun + verbEach student has a card.
everyall members, no exceptionssingular noun + verbEvery student is ready.

Never: every of the students. Say every student or every one of the students.
Theory · 9/10

Exactly two: both / either / neither

Both = the two

both answers

Both answers are correct.

Оба ответа.

Either = one or the other

either answer

Either answer is acceptable.

Любой из двух.

Neither = not one and not the other

neither answer

Neither answer is correct.

Ни один из двух.

With pronouns/determiners: both of them, either of the routes, neither of my parents.
Meaning trap: I don’t like either answer = I like neither answer. Do not add another negative after neither.
Theory · 10/10

no / none / neither

FormWhat follows?Example
noa nounThere is no information.
noneno noun; replaces itI asked for help, but none was available.
none ofthe/these/my/us/themNone of the students knew.
neitherexactly twoNeither option works.
Number rule: two → neither; three or more → none.
One negative is enough: I have no money = I don’t have any money. Standard English avoids I don’t have no money.

Practice · 2/10

Is it enough?

Use context, not only the noun.

01 I have ___ questions, if you have time. (some questions)
A few = some; a neutral/positive amount.
02 There is ___ hope of finding the documents now. (almost none)
Little = almost no hope.
03 Add ___ salt to improve the flavour. (some)
A little = a small but useful amount.
04 ___ witnesses remembered the number plate, so the case was difficult. (not enough)
Few carries the negative idea “not enough”.
Practice · 3/10

Choose the speaker’s attitude

01 We still have a little time, so let’s check the answers.
The article in a little makes the message positive.
02 Unfortunately, few candidates reached the final.
Few, reinforced by unfortunately, is negative.
03 Quite a few parents supported the proposal.
Quite a few surprisingly means a fairly large number.
04 We have only a little evidence.
Only makes the small amount sound insufficient.
Practice · 4/10

Repair the exceptions

Choose the grammatically correct version.

01 ___ live abroad.
Before my/the/these/us, use of: a few of my friends.
02 This version contains ___.
Countable plural errors → fewer in careful English.
03 The website provides ___.
Information is uncountable and has no plural here.
04 Before the interview, I felt ___.
A little + adjective means “slightly”; it is an adverbial use.

Practice · 8/10

Each, every or all?

01 The teacher spoke to ___ student separately.
Separately highlights students one by one: each.
02 ___ participant must show an ID. No exceptions.
Every + singular noun emphasizes no exceptions.

Practice · 9/10

Two choices

01 Anna and Max passed. ___ students succeeded.
Both = the two of them.
02 You can take the bus or the train. ___ route is fine.
Either = one or the other; both alternatives are acceptable.
03 I tried two keys, but ___ key opened the door.
Neither = not the first and not the second.
04 Both answers ___ possible.
Both takes a plural verb.
Practice · 10/10

No, none or neither?

01 There is ___ milk in the fridge.
No comes directly before a noun.
02 “How much milk is left?” “___.”
None replaces the noun.
03 ___ the five solutions worked.
There are five solutions, so use none of.
04 ___ the two solutions worked.
There are exactly two solutions, so use neither of.
05 Choose standard English.
Standard English normally uses one negative: not … any or no.
